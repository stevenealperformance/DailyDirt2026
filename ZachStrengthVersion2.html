Why these changes

  1. Push-to-pull rebalanced. Original was 36 push : 12 pull (3:1 push-heavy). Revised is 11 push : 24 pull (roughly 1:2 pull-biased). For a rider who already spends hours in a forward, kyphotic position, this is the single most important fix.
  2. Total volume reduced. The original ran ~170+ working sets per week. The revised version is ~95–100. For a young athlete training around riding, less is more — quality of execution beats raw set count, and recovery is what makes adaptation happen.
  3. Hinge variety added. Trap-bar deadlift gives a heavy bilateral hinge that the original was missing (RDL alone doesn't hit the same loading). Single-leg RDL fills the unilateral hinge gap — a high-value lift for any rider.
  4. Core overhauled. All trunk flexion replaced with anti-rotation (Pallof press), anti-extension (dead bug), anti-lateral-flexion (side plank, suitcase carry). On a bike, the spine's job is to resist forces, not flex repeatedly under load.
  5. Power consolidated to one day. Cleans were on both leg days, which is a lot of high-CNS-cost lifting twice a week. Now Day 1 is strength, Day 3 is power. Cleaner adaptation, easier recovery.
  6. Day 4 trimmed. Five bicep / tricep variations consolidated to one of each. Time freed up goes to two more pulling movements (pull-ups, bird dog DB row) and a farmer's carry. The bird dog row is a particularly good fit — it gets a unilateral horizontal pull and anti-rotation core in one movement, so it earns its slot twice over.
  7. What stayed the same. Four-day structure, two leg days with different emphases, RDL, back squat, front squat, Bulgarian split squats, box jumps, cleans, all the grip/forearm work, shin curls, back extension. The bones of his program were good — this is a rebalancing, not a rewrite.
